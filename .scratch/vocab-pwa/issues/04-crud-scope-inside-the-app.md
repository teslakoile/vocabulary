# CRUD scope inside the app

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: —

## Question

Three different jobs hide under "CRUD-able". Which of them does the app itself need to do, and what does each look like?

1. **Capture.** You hear a word in a meeting and want it in the list within about five seconds. Google Keep does this for you today. Does the PWA take this over, or does Keep stay as a capture inbox that the app drains?
2. **Correction.** You are mid-review, a definition or a cue is wrong or bland, and you fix it in place. This one almost certainly belongs in the app, because the review screen is where you notice the problem. The open part is how deep the edit goes: fix one field inline, or open a full entry editor.
3. **Bulk enrichment.** Generating definitions, forms, register notes, and cues across many entries at once. This is a batch job, not CRUD, and it may belong in a Claude Code session rather than on a phone.

The failure mode to design against: if enrichment lives outside the app, a word captured on Tuesday sits with no content until Kyle next sits at a desk. That is exactly the dead-list problem Google Keep has today, and shipping it again would mean the app has not fixed the real problem. Whatever is decided here must have an answer for a freshly captured, empty entry.

Also settle: what delete means. Is a removed entry gone, or archived with its review history intact?

## Answer

**Capture moves into the app. The app owns everything.** The 84 entries are imported once, the Google Keep note is archived, and all new words are captured in the PWA. There is one source of truth. This was taken over the strongest objection on the ticket (habit migration off Keep is the highest-risk part of the whole project) and the objection stands as a residual risk below.

**Content is LLM-generated at capture time.** A newly captured word gets its definition, register note, forms, and cues generated by a model, not by a batch pass in a Claude Code session. Two consequences, both binding on other tickets:

- This **rules out the "no runtime LLM" option** in [Stack, hosting, and LLM key custody](06-stack-hosting-and-llm-key-custody.md). A model must be reachable from the phone, so an API key must live somewhere reachable from the phone. That ticket is narrowed to two shapes: a server-side function holding the key, or bring-your-own-key in device storage.
- There is **no human review gate** on generated content. Content goes straight from the model into the review rotation.

**Capture never blocks on the network.** *(Assumption stated at resolution, not asked.)* The bare entry saves locally and instantly; generation is queued and retried when connectivity returns. Capture on a train with no signal must succeed, or the app is worse than Keep at the one thing Keep does well.

**A bare entry stays out of review until generation lands.** *(Assumption stated at resolution, not asked.)* It appears in browse with a pending marker and contributes to a pending badge. A failed generation must never surface as a blank card mid-session.

**Correction is inline edit.** Tap a field on the review screen, edit it, save, and carry on without losing your place in the session. No flag-for-later queue, which avoids the graveyard failure mode. Inline edit is therefore the *only* correction path, which makes it load-bearing: generate, surface in review, fix in place. That loop is the design.

**Browse is a first-class screen.** A searchable, readable list of all entries, separate from the review flow. It serves the first stated use case, which is remembering which words you have collected, and it is what you reach for while drafting a message. What you can search and filter by is a data-model question and belongs to [Entry and review-state data model](07-entry-and-review-state-data-model.md).

**Delete is archive, not destroy.** An archived entry stops appearing in review and drops out of the default browse view. Its review history is retained and it can be restored. Modelled as a single `archived` state rather than separate archive and deactivate states; say so if you meant two distinct things.

### Residual risks

- **Capture habit migration is the top risk on this map.** If opening the PWA is slower than opening Keep, you will keep using Keep, the app's list goes stale, and the app dies. Mitigation to investigate: whether iOS supports the Web Share Target API, which would let you capture from the system share sheet. Added as a fifth research area on [iOS PWA capability constraints](02-ios-pwa-capability-constraints.md). Earliest cheap signal: two weeks after the app is usable, count how many new words went into the app versus Keep.
- **No review gate means bland content can persist.** The inline-edit loop only works if you actually fix things when you notice them. If you find yourself skipping past a weak definition three times, the generation prompt is wrong and needs fixing at the source, not one entry at a time.
- **Generation at capture time adds a runtime dependency** on a model API, a key, and eventual connectivity, for what is otherwise an offline-capable app.

## Amended: generation is no longer at capture time (2026-08-23)

Kyle's call: content is generated once, in a Claude Code session, not by the app calling a model. See [Generation prompt](12-generation-prompt.md).

**This reverses the answer above.** "Content is LLM-generated at capture time" no longer holds. Everything else on this ticket stands: the app still owns capture, Keep is still archived, correction is still inline edit, browse is still first class, and delete is still archive.

**The consequence is the risk this ticket was written to kill.** A word captured on the phone now sits at `status: bare` until Kyle next runs a generation session at a desk. That is the dead-entry problem: a word in a list with nothing attached to it, which is exactly what Google Keep already does to him.

Mitigations already in the design, which now carry more weight than when they were written:

- **`capture_note` becomes materially more valuable.** Kyle's own few words at capture are the only content a bare entry has, and unlike everything else they need no session to exist. Still optional and still never blocking, but the case for prompting gently is stronger now.
- **The pending badge is now load-bearing, not cosmetic.** It is the only thing that will make Kyle notice bare entries have accumulated.
- **Browse's `Pending` filter is the queue** he works from when he does sit down.

**Signal to watch:** if bare entries routinely sit for more than a couple of weeks, this decision is not working and capture-time generation goes back on the table, most likely as a Worker route rather than a full reversal.
